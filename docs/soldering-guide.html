Solder the amp, the speaker and the three buttons

Two separate jobs. Job A gives the jukebox its sound. Job B adds the PLAY, PAUSE and STOP switches and can wait for another day. Everything is already on your bench; there is nothing to buy.

Set up the bench

Station

The only skill you need

One good joint

Every joint in this guide is the same four moves. First tin each wire: strip 4 mm, twist the strands, dab flux, then touch iron and solder to it until the strands turn silver.

GoodShiny, shaped like a small tent, wire outline still visible.
ColdDull grey ball sitting on the pad. Add flux and reheat.
StarvedBare copper showing. Reheat and feed a little more.
BridgeSolder touching a neighbour. Flux, then lift it off with the wick.

Job A · sound

Wires and amp

No breadboard, and nothing needs holding. For this job the speaker lies on the mat with the cone facing up, and the amp hangs from its four wires. A bare speaker sounds thin and quiet. That is normal: it needs the enclosure behind it.
  1. A1

    Find 5 V and ground with the meter

    Plug one pigtail into P1 and one into P3. The plugs are keyed. Strip 4 mm from all eight wire ends and tin them.

    • Spread all eight wire ends apart on the mat so none can touch another. Tape them down.
    • Plug in USB. Fluke on DC volts. Red probe on the wire you think is pin 1, black probe on the wire you think is pin 4.
    • About +5 V means you have them right. About −5 V means they are the other way round. Mark the 5 V wire with red tape and the ground wire with black.
    • Unplug USB.
    • Fluke on continuity. Find the P3 wire that beeps against your P1 ground wire. That is P3 pin 1. Counting from it: pin 3 is IO22, pin 4 is IO21.
  2. A2

    Solder the screw terminal to the amp

    Push the 2-pin terminal block into the two large holes marked + and −, wire openings facing off the edge of the board. Turn the board over, hold the block flat with tape, solder both legs. These are big joints: three seconds each is fine.

  3. A3

    Solder four wires into the amp

    • Cut the red, black, yellow and green pigtail wires to about 12 cm.
    • Push each tinned end down through its hole from the chip side: red → Vin, black → GND, yellow → BCLK, green → LRC.
    • Bend each wire over slightly underneath so it stays put, solder on the underside, then snip the excess flush.
    • Vin and GND are neighbours. Look at that pair under the lamp for a bridge.
Job A · sound

Speaker

  1. A4

    Solder two wires to the speaker lugs

    • Cut 12 cm of red and 12 cm of black 22 AWG. Strip 4 mm at one end of each and tin it. Strip 5 mm at the other end and leave it bare.
    • Put a small bump of solder on each speaker lug.
    • Lay the tinned wire end on the bump, touch the iron, let it sink in, lift. Red on the lug marked +.
    • Two seconds per lug. The hair-thin braid that feeds the cone is soldered to the same lug and lets go if you linger.
    • Twist the bare ends, push them into the amp's screw terminal, red in +, and tighten. Tug each one.
Job A · sound

The one board joint

The audio data line has no socket pin, so the blue wire goes straight onto the ESP32 module. The module is the metal can on the back of the board. Along its long edges sit rows of small gold pads, 1.27 mm apart. You need one of them.

  1. A5

    Find pad 12 and prove it

    Fluke on continuity. Count pads from the antenna end.

    • Pad 1 beeps against the metal USB socket shell. So does pad 1 on the other side, so this alone does not pick the side.
    • Pad 3 beeps against your yellow wire (IO22). On the wrong side it stays silent. This picks the side.
    • Pad 6 beeps against your green wire (IO21). Now the count is confirmed twice.
    • Count on to pad 12. Mark the can beside it with a marker dot.
  2. A6

    Solder the blue wire to pad 12

    • Cut a blue Dupont jumper to a bare wire about 12 cm long. Strip 2 mm, twist, tin.
    • Flux on pad 12. Touch the corner of the tip to the pad with a trace of solder for one second. It leaves a tiny bump.
    • Hold the wire with tweezers, lying flat and pointing away from the module. Rest its tinned end on the bump, touch the iron for one second, lift, hold still.
    • The solder must not reach the can or the pads either side. Tape the wire to the board 1 cm from the joint so nothing can lever it off.
    • Push the other end into the amp's DIN hole and solder it like the rest.
Job A · sound

Check, then power

  1. A7

    Six beeps before any power

    USB still unplugged, Fluke on continuity. Only a wrong answer on the first row can damage anything.

    ProbeProbeExpectIf wrong
    amp Vinamp GNDsilentA bridge between the two end pads. Wick it off. A half-second chirp is normal.
    amp GNDUSB socket shellbeepBlack wire is on the wrong P1 pin, or a cold joint.
    amp DINmodule pad 12beepReflow whichever end of the blue wire looks dull.
    module pad 12pads 11 and 13, and the cansilentBridge at the module. Flux, wick, redo with less solder.
    amp DINamp BCLKsilentBridge between neighbours on the amp.
    amp BCLKamp LRCsilentSame.

    Across the two speaker terminals the meter does beep. That is the 4 Ω coil and it is correct.

  2. A8

    Plug in and play

    Plug the board back into the Mac Studio, as it has been all day, so I can watch its log. Tap a channel, tap an episode. Sound should start within about three seconds, and the board's green LED flickers while it plays. Try the − and + buttons on the screen.

    What you getWhat it means
    Silence, LED flickeringData is arriving. Measure 5 V between amp Vin and GND, then check the terminal screws.
    Silence, LED dark, time counting upThe blue wire is not on IO16. Recount to pad 12.
    Noise or garbled soundYellow and green are swapped. Swap them at the amp.
    Crackle when you touch a wireA cold joint. Reflow that wire's two ends with flux.
    Screen restarts when it gets loudThe USB port cannot supply the peaks. Turn it down for now and tell me; the finished jukebox runs from a 5 V 3 A adapter.
    Then tell me what you hear. Clean, quiet, distorted or nothing: each one points at a single cause, and level is a firmware change on my side.
Job B · buttons

Switch harness

Wait for the switch holder before you solder this job. The enclosure does not yet have the part that holds the three switches under the top buttons, and the wire lengths here depend on it. The Pause and Stop buttons on the screen do the same job until then. To try real switches sooner, push them into a breadboard across its centre gap instead of soldering their legs.

Do this after Job A plays. All of the switch soldering happens off the board, on the mat. Unplug the P3 pigtail while you work on its black wire if that is easier.

The microSD slot stays empty from now on. The three button lines are the card slot's own pins, and a card in the slot would fight the buttons.
  1. B1

    Build the chain

    • Cut three thin signal wires, about 14 cm, in three colours. Flag the free ends with tape: PLAY, PAUSE, STOP.
    • Cut two black links of about 4 cm.
    • On each switch, solder the signal wire to one corner leg. Lay the tinned wire along the leg and heat both.
    • On the opposite corner leg of each switch, solder the ground: the black P3 pin 1 wire to the first switch, then a link on to the second, then a link on to the third. The middle legs take two wires in one joint.
    • Slide heat-shrink over each leg joint and shrink it with the hot barrel of the iron held close, not touching.
  2. B2

    Beep each switch before it goes near the board

    One probe on the black P3 wire, the other on a signal wire's free end. Silent when released, beep when pressed. Repeat for all three.

Job B · buttons

Three board joints

  1. B3

    Solder the signal wires to pads 9, 8 and 2

    Same module edge and same small joint as step A6. Use the pad map above and count from the antenna end again.

    SwitchPadPinWatch for
    PLAY9IO18Pads 8 and 9 are neighbours. Solder pad 9 first, check it, then pad 8.
    PAUSE8IO19Pad 7 beside it is unused, so a slip that way is harmless.
    STOP2IO23Pad 1 beside it is ground. A bridge there is a STOP button held down for ever.
    Keep away from pads 4 and 5. They are the serial lines I use to flash and talk to the board.

    Lead each wire straight off the module edge and tape all three down together 1 cm out.

    Plug in and press

    Start an episode from the screen, then:

    PressThe jukebox should

    PLAYPick the same episode up where it stopped.
