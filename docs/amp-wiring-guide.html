What you are building

Digital audio leaves the ESP32 on three I2S lines. The MAX98357A turns that into speaker power. No analogue wiring, no volume pot in the path.

Check those two twice. the odd oneFive wires go to sockets. One, DIN, is soldered to a pad because the board has no spare socket pin that can drive it. That pad is the only soldering.
Why GPIO 16 for DINThe free socket pins are IO21, IO22 and IO35. IO35 is input-only, so it cannot be the third I2S line. GPIO 16 drives the green element of the RGB LED and is the easiest reachable output. The LED will flicker with the audio data, which is harmless and even a nice sign of life.

Where things are on the back of the Sunton

Schematic view, component side up, USB socket at the bottom. Socket positions are approximate; go by the silkscreen labels P1, P3, CN1 and the three-colour LED. Pin order inside each socket is exact and is what matters.

Steps

  1. Unplug the Sunton from USB.

    Keep it unplugged until the Test section.

  2. Prepare the two pigtails.

    Take two of the 1.25 mm 4-pin pigtails from the Sunton box. Cut the free ends to about 15 cm, strip 4 mm, tin the ends. Plug one into P1 and one into P3. The plugs are keyed and only go in one way.

  3. Identify the wires by pin position, not colour.

    Pigtail colours vary between batches. With the plug in P1, the wire on pin 1 is VIN and the wire on pin 4 is GND. In P3, pin 3 is IO22 and pin 4 is IO21. Pin 1 is the one at the end marked on the silkscreen; if in doubt, beep from the wire end to the GND castellation on the module to find GND, then count.

  4. Find the GPIO 16 pad.

    Beside the RGB LED are three small resistors. Set the Fluke to continuity. One probe on castellation 27 of the ESP32 module (counting from the antenna end, left side as drawn; it is the GPIO 16 pin). Touch the other probe to each resistor end until it beeps. That end is your solder point.

  5. Solder the blue wire to that pad.

    Flux, a dab of solder on the pad, press the tinned wire in, reflow for a second. Pull gently to confirm it holds. Keep the wire short and route it away from the antenna end.

  6. Connect the five wires to the amp.

    The breakout has labelled solder pads: VIN, GND, SD, GAIN, DIN, BCLK, LRC. Solder red to VIN, black to GND, yellow to BCLK, green to LRC, blue to DIN. Leave SD and GAIN empty.

  7. Attach the speaker.

    Speaker leads into the amp's two-pin screw terminal, or soldered to its + and − pads. Polarity does not matter with a single speaker.

No-solder shortcut, if you want sound today without the iron

The Sunton has its own small amplifier wired to the SPEAK socket, fed from the ESP32's built-in 8-bit DAC on GPIO 26. Plug a speaker into that socket with a 2-pin 1.25 mm pigtail and I switch the firmware to drive it, one build flag. The quality is noticeably rougher than the MAX98357A, with hiss on quiet passages, so it is a stopgap, not the design. It needs no other wiring at all.
