# 004 - Touch navigation, three physical top buttons

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Status:** accepted. Supersedes the knob half of 001 (two encoders plus OK/PAUSE/STOP).
**Context:** The enclosure Praveen approved (concept D, a small wedge) has no room for two knobs: "that doesn't even fit correctly the knobs... should we just make it touchscreen?" He still wants real buttons for the three things a listener does without looking: "we still need play, pause and stop buttons", and to reuse parts on hand: "let's use those 4 prong buttons we already have, we can 3d print the buttons that sit outside on the device".
**Decision:** The screen's resistive touch does all navigation: tap a channel, tap an episode, Back in the header, a gear for Settings, a "Playing" pill that returns to Now Playing. Lists page with up and down arrows on a right-hand rail (four channels or three episodes a page); nothing scrolls or swipes, because resistive touch does drags badly and a 60-year-old should never lose a list to a stray flick. Every target is at least 10 mm. Now Playing carries four touch buttons: back 30 s, volume down, volume up, forward 30 s. On top of the case sit three 12 mm tactile switches under printed caps: PLAY (resume the last episode), PAUSE (toggle), STOP (save the place, return to Channels from anywhere; hold 3 s for Settings). They wire to GPIO 18, 19 and 23, the unused microSD socket lines, switch to ground with internal pull-ups.
**Alternatives:** Keep the knobs and grow the case (rejected by Praveen on size and looks). Touch only, no buttons (rejected: pause and stop must work by feel, and a locked-up touch layer must not strand the user). Swipe scrolling (rejected, see above). A capacitive panel (not owned; the Sunton board in stock is resistive).
**Consequences:** Volume is a stepped control on one screen rather than a knob that is always live. Touch calibration is per panel: the constants in `display.cpp` are for bench board 1 and a second board needs its own corner readings. Taps never run app code inside LVGL callbacks; they queue an input event the app handles in `loop()`, so blocking work (HTTP, starting a stream) cannot re-enter the UI.
