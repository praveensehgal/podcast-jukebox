# 006 - Two-part enclosure: body printed on its back, lift-out face plate carrying the screen

**Date:** 2026-10-11
**Status:** accepted
**Context:** Enclosure D was approved as a look-and-feel shell (2026-10-10) and printed as one piece, face down, with nothing inside to hold the speaker, the three switches or the Sunton. The real speaker measures 28.2 mm deep (the shell was drawn around 20), its magnet is about 40 mm across, and the switches' stems reach the magnet. Printing the one-piece shell face down also hung the whole underside in mid-air, which is where the first print strung.
**Decision:** The wedge becomes 114 x 73 x 67 mm (5 mm deeper) and splits into a body and a face plate, plus a switch bracket, three caps and a stem-cutting jig (`enclosure/build_d.py`, numbers in `enclosure/geom.py`).

- The body prints lying on its back wall. Everything fixed to it then stands straight up off the bed: the square speaker seat with four M3 bosses at the frame's corner holes, two posts for the switch bracket either side of the speaker, two pegs for the amp, and the floor is closed. The face frame is a 28 degree roof over nothing wider than its own thickness.
- The face plate is the black-glass outline, printed flat, face down. The Sunton sits on four posts behind its window with stops around three corners. Two tongues past its bottom edge slide under the skin below the opening; a fin behind that edge carries a foot that stands on the floor behind the board and takes two M3 screws from underneath. The plate goes in bottom first, tilted about 30 degrees, and swings flat.
- Three 12 mm switches sit in pockets on one bracket screwed to the two back-wall posts. Their stems are cut from 15 to 8.5 mm with the jig; the caps press onto the cut stems from outside, a rib on each cap keeps its icon upright, and a flange under the top wall keeps it captive.
- M3 screws form their own thread in 2.5 mm holes (no inserts).

The build script refuses to write files unless every part clears every other in the assembled position, the plate assembly swings in without touching, and the floor screws have thread in the foot.
**Alternatives:** Keeping the one-piece face-down shell with a base plate (the back wall becomes a 62 degree roof that needs supports and hides the screws behind the screen). Printing bottom-down (the top is a 38 mm bridge and every boss on the back wall is a 90 degree overhang). Hooking the plate at its top edge (the foot, 16 mm deep, sweeps into the front edge of the opening as the plate swings; checked numerically, it collides from 15 degrees down). A rail behind the tongues (anything hung behind the thin skin under the opening starts in mid-air when the body prints on its back). Moving the buttons or speaker sideways (the centre button still lands over the magnet).
**Consequences:** Every number for the Sunton other than its outline is nominal (hole positions 3 mm in from the corners, 9.8 mm stack, active area centred on the PCB), so the first print is a fit test and the post pattern may move. The plate's bottom corners rest only on the skin's edge; the bottom lip was left out because it could not be printed. The slicer must not add supports (the body's only unsupported spans are the 2 mm bridge where the cavity's front-bottom corner closes and the tops of the button holes). Buttons print on a separate plate so they can be printed one at a time.
